Google Ads for Water Damage Restoration

Plan water damage Google Ads around service intent, real coverage, call handling, qualified leads, and cost per booked job.

Paid search can put a restoration company in front of urgent demand, but a click is only the start. The campaign must match the service offered, the area a crew can reach, and a team able to answer the call.

Separate emergency and planned work

Keep emergency water extraction, sewage cleanup, flood cleanup, and commercial response distinct when volume supports it. Each group needs its own ad message and landing page. A homeowner facing a burst pipe should not land on a broad “all restoration services” page, while a facilities manager may need documentation and vendor information.

Start with queries that clearly describe a service you perform. Review the search terms report and exclude job seekers, training searches, DIY research, products, and locations outside the response area. Negative keywords are a maintenance practice, not a one-time list.

Use a landing page that answers the first call

Put the phone action, actual service area, hours, and response process at the top of the mobile page. Explain what happens after the call, what information helps dispatch, and what qualifications or proof the company can verify. Avoid implying insurance coverage is guaranteed. Test the click-to-call button and form on a phone.

Set budget from capacity and margin

Work backward from the number of jobs crews can handle, the acceptable acquisition cost per booked job, and the rate at which qualified calls become jobs. A $5,000 monthly spend that produces 50 calls and 10 booked jobs costs $100 per call and $500 per booked job before agency fees. Whether that works depends on gross margin and fulfillment capacity, not a generic industry benchmark.

Protect budget when dispatch is understaffed. If calls cannot be answered after hours, adjust scheduling or arrange a reliable intake path before buying more clicks.

Import outcomes, not just clicks

Track calls from ads and the website, but distinguish a phone tap from an actual conversation. Tag leads as qualified, booked, won, or lost in the CRM. Where the setup supports it, import qualified or booked outcomes to Google Ads so optimization has a signal closer to revenue. Review terms, call recordings where lawful, missed calls, and job values together.

Action checklist

  1. Group ads by real service and intent.
  2. Target only reachable areas.
  3. Review search terms and exclusions weekly.
  4. Test landing-page calls and forms.
  5. Calculate cost per qualified call and booked job.

Common questions

Should a new water damage company use Google Ads before SEO?

Paid search may create visibility sooner, while SEO builds over time. Start only when the response process, budget, and tracking can support paid calls.

Why are clicks rising but jobs flat?

Check search terms, location fit, call answer rate, lead qualification, and the landing page before increasing bids.
