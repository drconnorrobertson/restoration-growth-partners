Water Damage Marketing Budget and Lead Economics

Calculate water damage marketing cost per qualified lead, booked job, acquisition cost, and contribution margin with a simple worksheet.

The useful water damage marketing budget is the amount you can spend to win profitable work that your team can fulfill. Start with job economics and a realistic conversion path, then set channel budgets.

Use one funnel definition

Record spend, inquiries, qualified leads, booked inspections or jobs, won jobs, revenue, and gross profit by channel. A qualified lead should have a service need, reachable location, and a legitimate decision path. Keep definitions stable across SEO, Google Ads, Local Services Ads, and referrals so comparisons mean something.

Calculate costs from actual outcomes

Cost per qualified lead = marketing spend ÷ qualified leads. Cost per booked job = marketing spend ÷ booked jobs. Customer acquisition cost may also include sales and agency costs, depending on the decision being made. Contribution after marketing = revenue minus direct job costs minus marketing spend. If spend is $6,000, 30 leads qualify, six jobs book, and four are won, the cost is $200 per qualified lead, $1,000 per booking, and $1,500 per won job. Those numbers are an example, not a performance benchmark.

Set a budget ceiling from margin

Estimate gross profit per typical job by service type, then decide how much of that profit can fund acquisition while leaving room for overhead and risk. Emergency mitigation, reconstruction, and commercial jobs often have different margins and sales cycles; do not blend them into one misleading average. New firms may choose a test budget; established firms can allocate more to proven areas. Neither should spend beyond dispatch capacity.

Review attribution carefully

Capture the original inquiry source in the CRM, but allow for repeat visits, offline referrals, and direct calls after an ad impression. Reconcile tracking numbers and forms with dispatch records monthly. Use booked and won work to guide decisions while treating channel attribution as an estimate, not perfect truth.

Action checklist

  1. Define qualified and booked consistently.
  2. Separate service lines and markets.
  3. Calculate lead, booking, and won-job costs.
  4. Compare acquisition cost with gross profit.
  5. Review missed calls and job capacity before raising spend.

Common questions

What should a water damage company spend on marketing?

There is no universal percentage. Use actual margin, capacity, growth goals, and the cost of acquiring booked work in your market.

Is a cheap lead always better?

No. A low-cost lead outside the service area or without a real job can be more expensive than a qualified lead that books.
