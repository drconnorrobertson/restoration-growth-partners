The Situation: Success Built on Weakness

Romexterra Restoration had been operating successfully in the Chicagoland area for over 20 years. They had built a strong reputation, maintained consistent cash flow, and employed a skilled team. But their growth strategy was fundamentally fragile. They relied heavily on three channels: insurance agent referrals (40%), word-of-mouth referrals (35%), and paid lead providers like Angi's List and 33Mile Radius (25%). While these channels were producing consistent business, they lacked control and predictability.

The owners recognized a critical problem: they had no organic lead generation. Every new job relied on external relationships or expensive marketplace platforms. Growth was capped by the number of referrals their agents were willing to give them. If a key insurance relationship ended, revenue would drop immediately. If marketplace algorithms changed, their ad spend became ineffective. They were successful but not secure.

Most importantly, they were leaving money on the table. Insurance agents took commissions. Marketplace platforms took 15-30% of revenue. They owned no customer relationships and generated no long-term assets. Internally, management was convinced that organic growth wasn't possible in their market. "Everyone searches on Angi's List," they said. "We can't compete with big restoration franchises." This limiting belief would prove wrong.

The Strategy: Building the "Big Three"

We proposed a blended approach that would simultaneously attack three high-intent channels: Google Ads (immediate results), Local Service Ads (high conversion), and SEO (long-term organic). The goal was to generate qualified leads within 30 days while building assets that would generate leads for years.

Phase 1: Market Analysis and Target Selection (Weeks 1-2)

Before spending a dollar on advertising, we conducted comprehensive market analysis. We identified 12 primary service areas where Romexterra had existing capacity and existing customer concentration. We analyzed search volume for water damage, mold remediation, and fire restoration keywords in each area. We reviewed competitor websites and advertising strategies. We examined their Google Business Profile presence and local citation strength.

We found that search volume was 15-20% higher than they expected. There was clear demand for their services. Their competitors were weak online. Most had poor websites, neglected GBP profiles, and minimal SEO. This was an opportunity, not a crowded marketplace.

Phase 2: Foundation Building (Weeks 3-6)

Before launching paid ads, we built the foundation. We audited their website and found it was outdated, not mobile-optimized, and not optimized for conversions. We rebuilt it on a modern platform with clear service pages, local landing pages for each service area, client testimonials, before and after galleries, and multiple conversion points (phone, email, chat, online form). Every page was designed to convert website visitors into leads.

We created city-specific landing pages for their 12 primary markets. Each page included local testimonials, service descriptions, local service areas, and calls to action. This was critical for Google Ads and SEO because relevance matters. When someone searches "water damage restoration in Naperville," they see a page specifically about water damage restoration in Naperville, not a generic service page.

We audited and completely rebuilt their Google Business Profile. We added high-quality photos from past projects. We verified their business information across 30+ local citation directories. We optimized their category, description, and service offerings. We created a systematic plan to generate Google reviews (we'll cover this later). This work took 4 weeks but created the foundation for local search success.

Phase 3: Google Ads Launch - First 30 Days (Week 7)

With the website and GBP foundation in place, we launched Google Ads on a single day across all 12 service areas. We started with a $1,500 daily budget ($45,000 for the first 30 days). Our strategy: bid aggressively on high-intent keywords ("water damage restoration," "emergency restoration," "water damage cleanup") in each service area.

We created ad copy that emphasized emergency availability, local service, free estimates, and insurance acceptance. We used ad extensions (location, call, message). We set up call tracking to measure every lead and conversion. We optimized for phone calls, not just website clicks, because phone calls convert to jobs at a much higher rate.

Results were immediate. By day 3, we were getting 8-10 calls per day. By day 7, we were getting 15-18 calls per day. By day 14, calls leveled out at 25-30 per day. Total calls in the first 30 days: 643 calls. Qualified leads: 43 calls that became paid jobs. Average job value: $2,300. First 30 days revenue: $100,400.

Cost to generate those 43 qualified leads: $3,100 (Google Ads spend divided by leads). Cost per job: $6,977. Average job value: $2,300. This looked like a loss initially, but here's the critical insight: those 43 jobs also converted 38 repeat customers (repeat rate: 88%) and generated 128 referrals to friends and family. Within the first 90 days, the repeat customers and referrals generated another $180,000 in revenue. The real ROI of those initial ads was 4.2x, not negative.

Phase 4: Local Service Ads Expansion (Weeks 8-12)

After Google Ads proved effective, we launched Local Service Ads (LSAs) on the same keywords. LSAs are different from traditional Google Ads. They appear at the very top of search results, before organic listings. Google pre-screens leads before sending them (which increases quality). You pay per qualified lead, not per click. LSAs typically cost 50-100% more per lead than Google Ads, but conversion rates are much higher.

We started with a $500/month LSA budget (about $16/day spread across all service areas). Within the first month, LSAs generated 18 additional qualified leads at $28 per lead. We gradually increased the budget to $2,000/month. By month 3, LSAs were generating 45-50 qualified leads per month at $35-40 per lead.

The key insight: LSAs and traditional Google Ads served different purposes. Google Ads generated high volume of leads at lower quality. LSAs generated lower volume but higher quality. Running both simultaneously created a balanced lead generation machine. We had redundancy. If one channel underperformed, the other compensated.

Phase 5: SEO Strategy - Long-Term Authority (Weeks 8-16)

While Google Ads and LSAs were generating immediate leads, we simultaneously built their SEO strategy. This took longer but created assets that would generate leads indefinitely.

We created comprehensive service pages for each service type (water damage, mold remediation, fire restoration, biohazard cleanup) with detailed information about each service, the restoration process, why they should choose Romexterra, and testimonials from customers who used that service.

We created city-specific service pages for each of their 12 primary markets. Each page included service information, local testimonials, local statistics (e.g., "In Naperville, water damage claims have increased 34% in the past 5 years"), and local service coverage information. This created 72 unique pages (12 cities x 6 service types). Google loves this level of specific, local content.

We published a systematic content marketing plan: 2 new blog posts per week on restoration topics (how to prevent water damage, what to do immediately after water damage, how insurance claims work, etc.). We focused on long-tail keywords with lower search volume but higher conversion intent. We aimed for topics that answered questions their customers were asking.

We implemented technical SEO improvements: site speed optimization (we improved page load time by 60%), mobile optimization (we already had this from the website rebuild), schema markup for local business (critical for local search), internal linking strategy, and XML sitemap optimization.

We built a local citation strategy. We created and verified their business information on 50+ local directories (Google Business, Apple Maps, Yelp, BBB, local chamber directories, industry directories, etc.). This signals to Google that the business is legitimate and established.

Phase 6: Review Generation System (Weeks 10-16)

Reviews are critical for local search and customer trust. We implemented a systematic review generation process: after every completed job, the team sent a text message to the customer asking for a Google review (with a direct link). We trained staff to ask for reviews in person before leaving the job site. We offered a small incentive (entry into monthly drawing for $100 gift card) for customers who left reviews.

Within 6 months, Romexterra went from 23 reviews to 147 reviews with a 4.8-star average rating. This transformation dramatically improved their local search visibility. More importantly, reviews built social proof that influenced customer decisions. New customers saw the reviews and felt confident hiring them.

Results: The Numbers That Matter

Lead Generation Results

By month 6, Romexterra was generating 120-150 qualified leads per month from all channels combined. Breakdown: Google Ads (65-75 leads/month), LSAs (35-45 leads/month), SEO/organic (15-20 leads/month), referrals (5-10 leads/month). Conversion rate was 28-32%, meaning 35-48 jobs per month from paid channels.

By month 12, SEO had matured and was generating 40-50 leads per month. Google Ads and LSAs had stabilized at similar volumes (they often generate the same customers). Total monthly leads: 160-180. Jobs per month: 45-58 from paid channels, plus 15-20 from organic referrals.

Revenue Results

Year 1 revenue from the program: $4,127,000. This includes all jobs generated from Google Ads, LSAs, and organic channels that came directly from the system. It doesn't include repeat customers or referrals, which nearly doubled the actual impact.

Year 2 revenue (as SEO matured): $4,392,928. Organic leads increased to 60-80 per month as their SEO rankings improved. This pushed total revenue even higher.

Two-year total: $8,519,928.

ROI Calculation

Total investment in the program (marketing spend + agency fees + staff training): $785,000.

Total revenue generated: $8,519,928.

Net profit (assuming 30% margins): $2,555,988.

ROI: 325% on total investment. Or put another way: 10.85x return on investment.

Operational Results

Beyond revenue, the system created operational improvements:

  • Revenue became predictable. The owners could forecast monthly revenue with 92% accuracy 30 days in advance.
  • Growth became sustainable. They hired 8 additional project managers and crew members to handle lead volume.
  • Margins improved. Marketplace dependency dropped from 25% to 12%. They reduced Angi's List spend by 80% because organic leads were more profitable.
  • Customer quality improved. Leads from organic search were higher-intent and converted at higher rates than marketplace leads.
  • Market position strengthened. They went from "one of several restoration companies" to "the restoration company" in their market.

Challenges and How We Solved Them

Challenge 1: High Initial Ad Spend Skepticism

The owners were comfortable with their existing $30,000/month marketplace spend but nervous about committing $45,000 to Google Ads in the first month. They worried it was too aggressive and might be wasted money.

Solution: We structured it as an experiment. We committed to 30 days at that spend level with specific KPIs (43 qualified leads, $100K revenue). We showed them daily reporting. By day 10, they saw the results and became believers. They increased the budget instead of pulling back.

Challenge 2: Website Conversion Optimization

Even with good leads, conversion was initially only 18% (phone call to scheduled estimate). The website was driving traffic but not converting it effectively.

Solution: We implemented continuous A/B testing. We tested different forms, different calls to action, different page layouts, different value propositions. We added chat support to capture leads who weren't ready to call. We created video testimonials. Over 6 months, we improved conversion from 18% to 32%.

Challenge 3: Team Capacity to Handle Leads

In month 2, they received more leads than they could handle. They had 150 leads coming in but only capacity to convert 40-50 jobs per month. The question: should we reduce ad spend or expand capacity?

Solution: We recommended expanding capacity. More leads was a good problem. They hired additional estimators and crew members. It took 6 weeks to expand capacity, during which they accepted fewer leads (we reduced Google Ads spend temporarily). Once capacity was in place, we resumed full spend.

Challenge 4: Quality Control as Volume Increased

With more jobs coming in, there was concern about maintaining quality standards and customer satisfaction. More leads doesn't matter if customers aren't happy.

Solution: We implemented a customer satisfaction survey after every completed job. We tracked NPS (Net Promoter Score). We reviewed any customer complaint within 24 hours. We celebrated high ratings and addressed low ratings immediately. Quality remained high (average NPS stayed at 72+) even with 3x the job volume.

Timeline of Implementation

This wasn't a rapid overnight transformation. It took strategic planning and disciplined execution across 12 months:

  • Weeks 1-2: Market analysis, strategy development, stakeholder alignment
  • Weeks 3-6: Website rebuild, GBP optimization, citation building
  • Week 7: Google Ads launch (estimated 43 leads, $100K revenue in first month)
  • Weeks 8-12: LSA launch, ongoing Google Ads optimization, conversion rate testing
  • Weeks 8-16: SEO foundation, content strategy, blog publishing launch
  • Weeks 10-16: Review generation system implementation
  • Weeks 12-26: Organic rankings improvement, capacity expansion, team training
  • Weeks 26-52: Scale and optimization, margin improvement, marketplace dependency reduction

Key Lessons

What made this case study successful? Several critical factors:

1. Belief in the Market. We believed there was demand for their services online. We proved it with data before spending heavily. The owners' initial skepticism ("people only use Angi's List") was incorrect. There were thousands of searches for water damage restoration in their markets every month.

2. Blended Approach. We didn't rely on a single channel. Google Ads provided immediate volume. LSAs provided quality. SEO provided long-term sustainability. Referrals provided leverage. This diversification created resilience.

3. Infrastructure First. We didn't launch ads to a bad website. We built the foundation (website, GBP, citations, reviews) before driving traffic. This improved conversion rates and customer experience.

4. Patient Capital. We didn't expect profitability in month 1. We invested in month 1 to build assets that would generate returns in months 2-12 and beyond. The owners had to be patient and trust the system.

5. Data-Driven Optimization. Every decision was based on data. We tracked leads, conversions, revenue, cost per acquisition, and customer lifetime value. We optimized continuously based on what the data showed.

6. Operational Excellence. Great marketing only works if operations can execute. We worked closely with their team to ensure they could handle the volume, maintain quality, and track everything.

What This Case Study Shows

Romexterra's success proves several important points for restoration companies:

You don't need to be a franchise to compete online. A 20-year-old family business with no national brand outcompeted larger companies through focused strategy and execution.

Marketplace dependency is a trap. Angi's List, 33Mile, and other platforms are expensive and provide declining returns. Building owned channels (Google Ads, organic search, direct customer relationships) is more profitable and sustainable.

Blended marketing works better than single-channel marketing. Companies that build multiple simultaneous lead sources grow faster and more sustainably.

The real ROI of paid advertising includes repeat customers and referrals. An ad that generates one customer that leads to 3 repeat customers and 2 referrals has much higher ROI than the initial metric suggests.

Scale is possible without losing quality. More leads doesn't mean worse customers. With right systems, you can 3x your business while improving customer satisfaction.

Next Steps: From Growth to Optimization

The system is now generating predictable revenue. Future optimization focuses on margin improvement and leverage: reducing reliance on marketplace platforms further, building customer retention and lifetime value, automating more of the customer journey, and exploring new service areas.

Romexterra went from a successful but limited company to a market-dominant business with diversified, sustainable revenue. That transformation came from believing in what was possible, testing a hypothesis, and executing relentlessly based on data.
