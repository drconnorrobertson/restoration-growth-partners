Setting Up a CRM for Your Restoration Company: ServiceTitan, JobNimbus, and Beyond

Choose the Right Software and Build Systems That Scale Without Losing Quality or Profitability

Published April 28, 2026 | 16 min read

Your restoration business grows from 5 jobs per month to 20 jobs per month. You're still tracking everything in spreadsheets and phone notes. You're missing follow-ups. Invoices are late. Scheduling is chaotic. You need a CRM. A good CRM (Customer Relationship Management system) is the backbone of scalable restoration business. It manages leads, converts to jobs, tracks progress, schedules work, generates invoices, manages customer communication, provides business intelligence. Without it, you're running on tribal knowledge and chaos. With it, you can scale to 100 jobs per month and still maintain quality and profitability.

This guide covers how to choose a CRM for your restoration company and implement it correctly. I'll compare ServiceTitan (industry standard for contractors), JobNimbus (rising competitor), and other options, then walk through implementation.

Why Restoration Companies Need CRM Systems

Restoration business has characteristics that require CRM: high lead volume (you need to track all leads), urgent timeline (jobs need scheduling fast), complex workflow (lead to estimate to job to invoice), safety and compliance (documentation critical), service area coverage (multiple technicians, multiple locations), insurance coordination (need to track adjuster communications), customer follow-up (generate reviews, request referrals).

A spreadsheet-based system breaks at 10-15 jobs per month. You need dedicated software to scale beyond that.

Part 1: CRM Comparison for Restoration Companies

ServiceTitan: Industry Standard for Contractors

ServiceTitan is built specifically for restoration, plumbing, HVAC contractors. Strengths: purpose-built for restoration, excellent job scheduling, integrated invoicing and payment, strong reporting, good mobile app, API integrations with other tools, excellent customer support. Weaknesses: expensive ($500-$1500/month), significant implementation time, steep learning curve, requires commitment to use fully. Best for: established restoration companies with 50+ employees, companies with multiple teams/locations, companies needing advanced reporting.

Key features: lead management (capture and qualify leads), estimates and quoting, job scheduling and dispatch, photo documentation, customer portal, invoicing and online payment, performance analytics, integration with Google Calendar and other tools. Industry-specific features: water damage workflows, fire damage workflows, mold remediation workflows.

JobNimbus: Growing Alternative

JobNimbus focuses on contractor operations and job management. Strengths: mid-range pricing ($150-$400/month), simpler interface than ServiceTitan, strong job organization, good mobile app, photo documentation built-in, growing integrations. Weaknesses: newer platform (less mature than ServiceTitan), fewer restoration-specific features, smaller user base (less peer support). Best for: small to mid-size restoration companies (5-50 employees), companies prioritizing simplicity over advanced features.

Key features: job and project management, team collaboration, photo and document organization, payment tracking, reporting and analytics. Less restoration-specific than ServiceTitan but covers fundamentals well.

Other Options

Housecall Pro: affordable ($200-$400/month), strong for small services businesses, good mobile app, limited enterprise features. Buildots: construction-focused, good for multi-project companies, strong reporting. Airtable or Monday.com: generic project management, customizable, requires setup. Zoho CRM: generic CRM, affordable, requires configuration for restoration workflows.

Part 2: Choosing the Right CRM for Your Company Size

Small Company (1-10 Employees)

Recommendation: JobNimbus or Housecall Pro. Start simple. You don't need advanced features yet. Simple system focused on job scheduling, customer communication, invoicing. Budget: $200-$400/month. Implementation time: 2-4 weeks. Focus: getting team to use system consistently, establishing workflows.

Growing Company (10-50 Employees)

Recommendation: ServiceTitan (if budget allows) or advanced JobNimbus implementation. You need better job assignment, reporting, performance tracking. Multiple teams need coordination. Budget: $500-$1,000/month. Implementation time: 4-8 weeks. Focus: establishing workflows that scale, training team thoroughly, integrating with accounting software.

Established Company (50+ Employees)

Recommendation: ServiceTitan or custom integration. You need advanced reporting, multiple team management, API integrations with other systems. Budget: $1,000+/month. Implementation time: 8+ weeks. Focus: optimization, advanced workflows, integration across systems.

Part 3: CRM Implementation Best Practices

Implementation Process

Phase 1 (Week 1-2): Setup and configuration. Create user accounts, configure settings, import existing customer data, setup workflows. Phase 2 (Week 2-3): Team training. Train on essential features, practice with test jobs, establish processes. Phase 3 (Week 3-4): Pilot. Run system in parallel with old system. Track data quality, identify issues. Phase 4 (Week 4+): Go live. Switch over completely, monitor closely, provide ongoing support. First month is critical. Don't abandon system if first week is chaotic. Every team has learning curve.

Data Migration

Migrate existing customer data: names, contact info, past jobs, amounts. Don't migrate decades of history. Migrate last 2-3 years useful data. Cleanse data before importing (remove duplicates, fix formatting). Import process takes time. Allocate 1-2 weeks. Assign person responsible. Verify data accuracy after import. Wrong data in system creates problems downstream.

Workflow Configuration

Map your business process into system: lead comes in (from Google, phone, form), gets qualified, estimate is created, job is scheduled, technicians perform work, invoice is generated, customer pays, follow-up for review. Configure CRM to match this flow. Customize fields to match your data needs. Less customization is better (complexity reduces adoption). Use system's defaults when possible.

Integration Setup

Connect CRM to other tools: Google Calendar (job scheduling), Slack (notifications), Zapier (automation), Quickbooks or Wave (accounting), Google Forms (lead capture). Integrations reduce manual data entry and improve efficiency. Start with 3-4 essential integrations. Add more as team matures.

Part 4: Driving CRM Adoption

Getting Team Buy-In

Team will resist new system. "Too complicated." "I liked the old way." "More work." Overcome this: explain why system matters (helps them do jobs better, faster), train thoroughly (don't assume they'll figure it out), make it easy (simple interface, clear instructions), reward usage (bonuses for high CRM activity), address concerns (listen to feedback, make adjustments). First month is hard. Month 2 gets easier. By month 3, system should feel natural.

Role-Specific Training

Different roles use system differently: office staff (lead entry, scheduling), technicians (job details, photo documentation), management (reporting, performance tracking), accounting (invoicing, payment tracking). Train each role on their specific tasks. Don't train everyone on everything. Focus on their job.

Ongoing Support and Optimization

After go-live, don't abandon system. Provide support (who answers questions?), optimize workflows (what's not working?), review data (are people using correctly?), add features (what's missing?). Monthly review: are we using CRM correctly, where are adoption issues, what can we improve? First 3 months requires hands-on management. After that, system runs on its own (mostly).

Part 5: Using CRM Data to Improve Business

Key Metrics to Track

Lead metrics: leads per month, lead source (Google, reviews, referral), conversion rate (leads to jobs), average time from lead to job. Job metrics: jobs per month, average job value, jobs per technician, repeat customer rate. Financial metrics: revenue per month, revenue per job, gross margin, invoice accuracy. Customer metrics: customer satisfaction, review generation rate, repeat customer rate.

Review metrics monthly. Which metrics are improving? Which declining? Adjust strategy based on data.

Using Data for Decision Making

CRM provides data to answer critical questions: which lead sources are most profitable? (double down on those), which technicians are most efficient? (learn from them), which service areas are most profitable? (focus there), what's our actual close rate? (improve sales process if low), are we profitable on average job value? (increase prices if margin low).

This data-driven management is what separates companies that grow profitably from companies that grow chaotically.

Common CRM Implementation Mistakes

Mistake 1: Choosing system without team input. They have to use it. Involve them. Mistake 2: Expecting immediate adoption. Takes time. Patience required. Mistake 3: Over-customizing. Complexity kills adoption. Mistake 4: Not cleaning data before import. Garbage in, garbage out. Mistake 5: No integration with accounting. CRM and accounting should talk. Mistake 6: No ongoing training. New staff need training. Mistake 7: Not monitoring adoption. If people aren't using, address immediately. Mistake 8: Not optimizing after go-live. System needs refinement based on real usage.
