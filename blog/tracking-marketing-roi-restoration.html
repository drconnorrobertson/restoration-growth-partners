How to Track Marketing ROI for Your Restoration Company

Complete system for tracking every marketing dollar and proving its impact on revenue and profit

Published April 30, 2026 | 16 min read

Most restoration company owners can't answer the question: "What's my marketing ROI?" They spend money on Google Ads, Angi's List, SEO, reviews, websites, but they don't actually know which channels are profitable. This lack of visibility is expensive. You might be pouring money into unprofitable channels while starving profitable ones. Or you might be killing profitable marketing because you can't see that it's working.

Tracking marketing ROI solves this problem. If you know precisely which marketing dollars generate which revenue, you can make intelligent decisions about where to invest more and where to cut. This chapter walks you through building a complete ROI tracking system for your restoration company.

Why Restoration Companies Struggle With ROI Tracking

Restoration marketing is complicated to track because customers don't come from single channels. A customer might see your Google Ad, visit your website, read your reviews, call your phone number, and hire you. Which channel gets credit? Google Ads led them there. Reviews convinced them. Your website converted them. If you credit all to Google Ads, you overestimate paid ads. If you don't credit Google Ads, you underestimate paid effectiveness.

Additionally, many restoration leads come from referrals and word-of-mouth. How do you track the channel that generated the original customer who referred their friend? It's complex.

Most restoration companies give up on tracking and make marketing decisions based on gut feel. "Google Ads seem to work." "We should do more Angi's List." These decisions are often wrong.

The ROI Tracking System

Step 1: Implement Phone Tracking

Every marketing channel needs to drive to a tracked phone number. Set up a phone service that provides separate numbers for each channel: one number for Google Ads, one for website, one for Angi's List. When customers call, you automatically know which channel they came from.

Services like CallRail, Call Tracking Metrics, or Twilio provide this functionality. Cost: $50-200/month depending on call volume. This is essential infrastructure.

Step 2: Track Calls to Revenue

Each tracked phone number should record calls. Not for invasive listening, but so you can verify your team actually closed the sale. More importantly, track these metrics for each call: date, phone number (source), did they schedule an estimate, did they become a customer, what was the job value.

Create a simple spreadsheet or use your CRM to record: Lead Source > Date > Phone > Scheduled > Revenue. Update this immediately after every job closes so data is accurate.

Step 3: Google Analytics Integration

If customers come through your website before calling, integrate phone tracking with Google Analytics. When a customer calls from your website (using a unique tracked number), Google Analytics records the session. This shows the full customer journey: Google Ad > Website > Phone Call > Revenue.

Use UTM parameters in all your ad links and website links to tag the source. Example: "utm_source=google&utm_medium=cpc&utm_campaign=water_damage_denver" tells you exactly where the traffic came from.

Step 4: CRM for Full Visibility

Implement a CRM (Customer Relationship Management) system like HubSpot, Zoho, Salesforce, or Pipedrive. Every lead that comes in gets logged in the CRM with source, dates, status, and revenue. This gives you complete visibility into your sales pipeline and where each customer came from.

ROI Calculation Framework

The Basic Formula

ROI = (Revenue from Channel - Marketing Spend on Channel) / Marketing Spend on Channel

Example: You spent $5,000 on Google Ads in April. Those ads generated 12 leads. 4 leads became jobs worth $25,000 total revenue. ROI = (25,000 - 5,000) / 5,000 = 400%.

More useful metric: Cost Per Acquisition (CPA) = Marketing Spend / Jobs Generated. In the example: CPA = 5,000 / 4 = $1,250 per job. If your average job is $6,250, your profit per job is $3,250 (assuming 50% margins), minus the $1,250 CPA = $2,000 net profit per job. This tells you Google Ads is profitable.

Account for Multi-Touch Attribution

Most customers don't convert on first touch. They might see an ad (touch 1), visit your website (touch 2), read reviews (touch 3), then call (touch 4). Which touchpoint gets credit?

Options: First-touch attribution (credit the first interaction), last-touch attribution (credit the last interaction before conversion), linear attribution (split credit across all touches). Most restoration companies use last-touch (they credit the phone call or final interaction).

Your choice depends on your business. For emergency services, last-touch makes sense because the final urgency pushed them to call. If you want to know which channels drive awareness, first-touch matters. Choose a model and stick with it for comparison over time.

Consider Customer Lifetime Value

Initial ROI on a customer acquisition is valuable, but lifetime ROI matters more. A customer acquired via referral for $0 cost who generates 3 jobs ($15,000 total) over 2 years has massive lifetime value. A customer acquired via expensive channel who generates 1 job ($6,000) has lower lifetime value despite higher initial revenue.

Calculate: Customer Lifetime Value = average job value x repeat rate x avg years as customer. Track this by channel. Some channels (referrals) produce higher LTV. Some channels (Angi's List) produce lower LTV. This should affect your investment decisions.

What Metrics to Track

Essential metrics for each marketing channel:

  • Marketing Spend (monthly and cumulative)
  • Leads Generated (total and qualified)
  • Cost Per Lead (Spend / Leads)
  • Conversion Rate (Leads / Jobs, typically 20-40%)
  • Cost Per Job (Spend / Jobs)
  • Revenue Per Job (your average job value from this channel)
  • Gross Profit Per Job (Revenue minus COGS)
  • Net Profit Per Job (Gross Profit minus Marketing Cost)
  • Repeat Customer Rate (% of customers who use you again)
  • Referral Rate (% of customers who refer others)
  • Customer Lifetime Value (total revenue from one customer over time)

Track these by channel monthly. Create a dashboard that shows all metrics at a glance. Update it weekly so you have current data for decision-making.

Common ROI Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake 1: Only Counting Direct Revenue. You see 10 leads from Google Ads that became 3 jobs, so you think ROI is low. But those 3 customers generated 2 referrals that became additional jobs. The true ROI includes the referrals. Always account for downstream benefits of initial acquisition.

Mistake 2: Only Counting Immediate Revenue. A customer you acquire today might call you again 2 years later for another job. Count lifetime value, not first-transaction value.

Mistake 3: Ignoring Brand Effect. Google Ads generate direct leads but also build brand awareness. People who don't respond to the ad might remember you and refer later. Track brand awareness separately from direct response.

Mistake 4: Comparing Channels Unfairly. Google Ads are immediate conversion. SEO takes 6 months to produce leads. If you compare ROI after 3 months, SEO looks terrible. Give channels adequate time to perform before judging.

Mistake 5: Not Tracking Source of Inbound Calls. If you don't have tracked phone numbers, half your inbound calls have unknown source. You're flying blind.

Tools to Implement ROI Tracking

Phone Tracking: CallRail, Call Tracking Metrics, Twilio. Essential. Cost: $50-200/month.

Analytics: Google Analytics (free) + UTM parameters. Essential. Cost: Free.

CRM: HubSpot (has free tier), Zoho, Pipedrive. Essential for complete visibility. Cost: $0-200/month.

Spreadsheet Tracking: Google Sheets or Excel with formulas to calculate ROI. Simple but effective. Cost: Free.

Dashboard: Data Studio (free) or Tableau to visualize metrics. Nice to have. Cost: $0-70/month.

The Monthly Review Process

Every month, conduct a marketing ROI review:

  1. Pull data from all sources (phone tracking, CRM, analytics, ad accounts)
  2. Calculate key metrics for each channel (spend, leads, CPA, CPS, revenue)
  3. Compare to previous months and year-over-year
  4. Identify highest and lowest performing channels
  5. Ask: which channels are profitable enough to scale? Which should we reduce? Which should we test?
  6. Make budget decisions based on data, not gut feel
  7. Implement changes and track results next month

ROI Tracking Drives Better Decisions

With complete ROI tracking, you can make intelligent decisions like: "Google Ads produce $2,000 net profit per job. Angi's List produces $800. Let's increase Google Ads budget by 50% and decrease Angi's by 30%." This is data-driven decision-making.

Without tracking, you make decisions like: "Let's try this new marketing tactic and hope it works." Hope is not a strategy.

Spend the time to implement tracking. It takes 2-4 weeks to set up completely. Once running, it gives you the visibility you need to make marketing investments that actually improve profit.
